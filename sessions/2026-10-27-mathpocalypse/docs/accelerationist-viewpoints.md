# The accelerationist case for AI in maths and science: a reading map

- **Purpose:** to place [*Solve Everything*](solve-everything-summary.md) (Wissner-Gross & Diamandis)
  within the wider techno-optimist and accelerationist tradition, so the session isn't relying on a
  single manifesto for the "pro" side.
- **Added:** 18 October 2026

> These are advocacy texts, and most of their authors run or fund AI companies. Summaries are
> paraphrased unless quoted. Items marked *(secondary)* come from press coverage or transcripts and
> have not been checked against the original.

## The short version

The camp shares one premise: **intelligence is turning into an abundant, cheap input, and
scientific progress has mainly been limited by the scarcity of it.** Members disagree on four
points, and those disagreements are more useful for discussion than the shared premise:

1. **Where the next bottleneck is.** Possible answers are routing and targets (*Solve Everything*),
   the physical world and institutions (Amodei), verification (Harmonic and the Lean community),
   or diffusion into the wider economy (Epoch/Mechanize).
2. **How fast.** Estimates range from "within months" (Szegedy) to 2027 (Aschenbrenner) to "a
   decade-plus" (Hassabis).
3. **Whether to steer.** Positions range from accelerating everything (Andreessen, e/acc) to
   accelerating selectively (Buterin's d/acc, Hassabis's "cautious optimist").
4. **What counts as knowing.** At one end, a result needs Lean-certified proof (Harmonic). At the
   other, "probability 1-ε" is good enough (Zeilberger).

One place where nearly all of them agree is that **maths goes first**: it needs no wet lab, no
regulator and no physical data, so it has the fewest bottlenecks other than thinking.

## 1. Lab leaders: "AI for science" as mission

These are the most influential voices, because they control the systems that produce the results.
They are more hedged than the manifestos.

### Demis Hassabis: "solve intelligence, then everything else"

- DeepMind's 2010 founding plan read: "Step one: Solve intelligence. Step two: Use it to solve
  everything else." *Solve Everything* takes its title and ambition from the same idea.
- AlphaFold, which shared the 2024 Chemistry Nobel, is the template both texts cite: an open,
  benchmarked problem (CASP) that was industrialised.
- In February 2026 Hassabis described "radical abundance" and a "new golden era of discovery" within
  10–15 years ([Fortune](https://fortune.com/2026/02/11/demis-hassabis-nobel-google-deepmind-predicts-ai-renaissance-radical-abundance/), *secondary*).
- At Stanford GSB in June 2026 he said the plan's "broad arcs" have gone "unbelievably well". He
  calls AGI "10 times the impact" of the Industrial Revolution, "10 times faster", and wants "the
  ultimate tool for science". He also called himself a **"cautious optimist"**, said "the public's
  right to be concerned", and criticised peers for being "way too certain"
  ([Stanford GSB](https://www.gsb.stanford.edu/insights/demis-hassabis-thinks-were-foothills-singularity);
  auto-transcript, so the timeline figures may be garbled).
- **For the session:** he is the optimist that mathematicians most readily accept. DeepMind tends to
  publish work with mathematicians, such as AlphaEvolve with Tao and Gómez-Serrano, rather than
  claim results on its own.

### Dario Amodei: *Machines of Loving Grace* (October 2024)

- "Powerful AI" is defined partly by maths: smarter than a Nobel laureate across fields, so that it
  can "prove unsolved mathematical theorems".
- His core image is a **"country of geniuses in a datacenter"** producing a **"compressed 21st
  century"**: 50–100 years of biological progress in 5–10 years.
- **Its most useful idea here is "marginal returns to intelligence".** Progress is limited by
  factors that extra intelligence can't replace: how fast the physical world moves, the need for
  data, intrinsic complexity, human institutions, and physical law. This disagrees with *Solve
  Everything*'s "Domain Collapse" story. It also explains why maths would move fastest, since
  almost none of those limits apply.
- The January 2026 companion essay, *The Adolescence of Technology*, covers the risks. Together the
  two essays give a "both at once" position: very large upside and very large risk.

### Sam Altman: cheap intelligence

- *The Intelligence Age* (September 2024) says superintelligence may arrive "in a few thousand
  days". *Three Observations* (February 2025) says the cost of a given level of AI falls about
  10× a year.
- [*The Gentle Singularity*](https://blog.samaltman.com/the-gentle-singularity) (June 2025) says
  "Scientific progress is the biggest driver of overall progress", "2026 will likely see the
  arrival of systems that can figure out novel insights", and "the cost of intelligence should
  eventually converge to near the cost of electricity". He calls AI-assisted AI research "a larval
  version of recursive self-improvement".
- **For the session:** the "novel insights in 2026" line is a dated prediction that the September
  and October OpenAI maths releases can now be checked against. Note that the person making the
  prediction is also the one marking it.
- In 2026 his tone moved towards politics, with an OpenAI policy paper proposing a "New Deal" for
  superintelligence
  ([Axios](https://www.axios.com/2026/04/06/behind-the-curtain-sams-superintelligence-new-deal),
  *secondary*).

## 2. Ideological accelerationism

### Marc Andreessen

- *Why AI Will Save the World* (June 2023) argues that AI augments human intelligence: every child
  gets a tutor and every scientist gets a collaborator. It treats AI safety worries as a moral
  panic.
- The [*Techno-Optimist Manifesto*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Techno-Optimist_Manifesto)
  (October 2023) says intelligence is the engine of progress. It says slowing AI costs lives, so
  that preventable deaths count as "a form of murder". It names "the precautionary principle",
  "trust and safety" and "risk management" as enemies. It ends with 56 "patron saints", including
  e/acc accounts and Nick Land.
- **For the session:** this is the purest "don't steer" position. It has nothing to say about
  verification, credit or how a field absorbs results, which is exactly where the maths debate is
  happening.

### e/acc (effective accelerationism)

- Started in 2022 by "Beff Jezos" (Guillaume Verdon, a former Google quantum researcher who
  founded Extropic) and "bayeslord". Its framing is thermodynamic: civilisation should maximise
  energy use and climb the Kardashev scale, and markets, not regulators, should decide direction.
- It is more a meme culture than a body of argument, but it shaped how Silicon Valley talks about
  this. *Solve Everything*'s "Dark Path", where safety concerns freeze progress, comes from this
  tradition.

### Nick Land

- Land's 1990s writing with the Cybernetic Culture Research Unit (CCRU) is the original source of
  "accelerationism". It treats techno-capital as a process that will leave humans behind.
- He belongs here as background, not as an optimist. Commentators note that Andreessen's
  pro-human reading of Land contradicts what Land himself argues
  ([The New World](https://www.thenewworld.co.uk/matthew-dancona-nick-land-the-dark-magus-of-ai/)).
  The "utopian" camp has a much darker branch.

## 3. Forecasters

- **Ray Kurzweil,** *The Singularity Is Nearer* (2024), repeats his "law of accelerating returns":
  human-level AI by 2029 and a merger of humans and machines by 2045. Diamandis co-founded
  Singularity University with him, so this is *Solve Everything*'s intellectual parent.
- **Leopold Aschenbrenner,** *Situational Awareness* (June 2024), counts "orders of magnitude" of
  compute and algorithmic gains to reach automated AI researchers around 2027, and then an
  intelligence explosion. It frames this as a US–China national-security race. It is the closest
  text to an engineering argument for the "everything at once" timeline.

## 4. Policy acceleration

- **America's AI Action Plan,** *Winning the Race* (July 2025), includes investment in AI-enabled
  science.
- **The Genesis Mission** executive order (24 November 2025) tells the Department of Energy to build
  an "American Science and Security Platform" combining national-lab supercomputers, data and AI
  agents. It must name at least 20 national science challenges and aim for robotic labs. The order
  compares its urgency to the Manhattan Project
  ([Morrison Foerster](https://www.mofo.com/resources/insights/251211-executive-order-establishes-genesis-mission);
  [ANS](https://www.ans.org/news/2025-11-26/article-7583/)). I couldn't confirm whether its 2026
  milestones were met.
- **For the session:** *Solve Everything*'s "Targeting Authorities" and "buy proofs, not hours"
  are close to what Genesis already does. The manifesto's policy ideas are less fringe than its
  tone suggests.

## 5. Voices from inside mathematics

These matter most for the session, because they are people the room will recognise.

### Christian Szegedy: maths "graduates" into an industry

- Szegedy (formerly at Google, where he worked on Inception, and a co-founder of xAI) has argued
  since his 2020 autoformalisation paper that this path leads to general AI. His essay is
  [*Quo Vadis, Mathematics?*](https://docs.google.com/document/d/e/2PACX-1vTSh-pyNP3Gi99WMmsinnLmE9V5CDI0HEm6WGbIPMNt3V5SGlClHF8-BetotKHOImrvQDSXmFdiOw8D/pub)
  (3 October 2026). It circulates as *Is Mathematics Over, or Just Graduating?*
  ([HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49963382)).
- His image is maths as exploring wilderness. AI is at the "Wright brothers" stage, but planes can
  already "fly into the Darién Gap". He calls objections based on today's limits a "static-thinking
  fallacy", and says they will be out of date "in a few months".
- He predicts that maths "will grow into a full-scale industry" and become "the true
  infrastructure of all scientific and technological progress". "The age of lone climbers is
  closing", and the skills that will count are choosing terrain and explaining results. He
  acknowledges the grief: "the old vibe and thrill will be gone forever", and the commercial phase
  will be "messy and dirty".
- **This is the most sympathetic accelerationist text for a maths audience**, and it is close to
  *Solve Everything*'s "Hero to Harness Builder" story told from inside the field. Like the
  manifesto, it **doesn't discuss verification**.

### Harmonic: "mathematical superintelligence", verified

- Harmonic was founded in 2023 by Vlad Tenev (CEO of Robinhood) and Tudor Achim. It was valued at
  $1.45bn in early 2026 and is still pre-revenue
  ([Index Ventures](https://www.indexventures.com/perspectives/solving-the-ai-reasoning-gap-how-harmonic-is-building-mathematical-superintelligence/);
  funding details *secondary*). Its stated plan is to solve major open problems, then move on to
  physics and computer science. That is *Solve Everything*'s maths-first cascade, backed by real
  money.
- Its key difference is that **verification is the strategy, not one layer among nine**. All
  output goes through Lean 4. See [the tools note](ai-mathematics-research-tools.md) and the
  [Harmonic Aristotle idea](../../../ideas/mathematics-harmonic-aristotle.md).

### Doron Zeilberger: accelerate, and drop the rigour

- Zeilberger (Rutgers) has argued for decades that computers will take over maths. In
  [Opinion 196](https://sites.math.rutgers.edu/~zeilberg/Opinion196.html) (15 June 2026) he
  predicts that "humans will only do math for fun", with perhaps "the top fifty 'math
  grandmasters'" staying professional. He hopes AI will be wiser than humans' "obsession with
  (alleged) *absolute certainty*", and that deep results will be known "with probability 1-ε".
- **He is the opposite of Harmonic.** Both are accelerationists, but one says rigour is what makes
  AI maths trustworthy and the other says rigour is a human hang-up. That split is the session's
  verification theme in miniature.

### Optimists among working mathematicians

- Gil Kalai's
  ["Be a Grothendieck!"](https://gilkalai.wordpress.com/2026/09/22/be-a-grothendieck-on-ai-and-mathematics/)
  (22 September 2026) leans enthusiast. He opposes slowing down AI-assisted maths while accepting
  that it brings real risks to the community. The post is also a good index of the September 2026
  essays on both sides.
- Examples he collects *(secondary)*:
  - Alexandre Eremenko reports that AI solved at least 7 of his roughly 40 long-open problems.
  - Ingo Althöfer says AI has opened his "golden years" in maths.
  - Chen & Klartag's AI-assisted proof of the sharp thin-shell inequality is praised for being
    *simpler* than earlier proofs, which is a reply to the "proof without understanding" worry.

## 6. Disagreements inside the pro-AI camp

These are what make the "pro" side more than one position.

- **Vitalik Buterin, d/acc** (*My Techno-Optimism*, November 2023). He is a techno-optimist who
  rejects e/acc's "don't steer". He would accelerate *defensive, decentralised and democratic*
  technology in particular, and warns that superintelligence is a distinct risk. Applied to maths,
  this suggests favouring open provers and open formal libraries over proprietary swarms, which
  links to the two-tier science worry.
- **Erdil & Barnett (Epoch, then Mechanize),**
  [*Most AI value will come from broad automation, not from R&D*](https://epoch.ai/gradient-updates/most-ai-value-will-come-from-broad-automation-not-from-r-d)
  (April 2025). They are accelerationists about automation, and went on to found a company aiming
  to automate all work. But they argue the "AI scientist solves everything" story is economically
  overrated: R&D accounts for only about a fifth of US productivity growth. Even if *Solve
  Everything* is right about maths, that may matter less than it thinks.
- **Amodei against "Domain Collapse".** Complementary factors make the speed-up smaller and uneven
  across fields. Read this way, maths is the *exception* to his limits, not proof that every field
  will collapse.
- **Hassabis against the manifestos.** He is a cautious optimist who says peers are "way too
  certain", wants "smart regulation", and worries about a "race to the bottom".

## Where *Solve Everything* sits

- **Shared with the camp:** cheap cognition, maths first, AlphaFold as the template, and abundance
  as the destination.
- **Distinctive:** the institutional design (L0–L5 maturity levels, Targeting Authorities,
  outcome procurement). Most accelerationist texts describe what will happen; this one tries to
  specify the machinery. It is closer to Genesis-style policy than to e/acc.
- **Weakest where the maths insiders are strongest:** its verification layer is generic, whereas
  Harmonic (Lean-first) and Zeilberger (probabilistic) both take a definite, opposite position on
  what checking means. Szegedy shares its blind spot.

## Possible discussion prompts

- Szegedy says objections based on today's limits are a "static-thinking fallacy". Which of the
  current objections, such as forcing loopholes, retrieval mistaken for novelty, or withdrawn
  manuscripts, are about today's limits, and which would apply at *any* capability level?
- Harmonic or Zeilberger: does AI maths need *more* rigour than human maths, or less?
- Amodei's "marginal returns to intelligence" says maths has few bottlenecks other than thinking.
  Is refereeing, community uptake or "understanding" a bottleneck he left out?
- Buterin's d/acc applied to maths: what would "defensive acceleration" look like? Open provers,
  open formal libraries, public Comparator-style checking?

## Caveats

- **Self-interest.** Hassabis, Amodei, Altman, Andreessen, Verdon, Tenev and Diamandis all have
  commercial stakes. Zeilberger, Kalai and, to a degree, Szegedy are the main voices without a
  company to sell.
- **Some recall is from memory.** Quotes from the 2023–2025 texts (Andreessen, Amodei, Altman's
  earlier essays, Aschenbrenner, Kurzweil, e/acc) come from my knowledge of the originals, not a
  re-read today. Check them before putting any on a slide. The 2026 quotes from Szegedy,
  Zeilberger, Hassabis at Stanford and *The Gentle Singularity* were checked against the source
  pages.
- **Not found:** any direct reaction from Andreessen or e/acc to the September 2026 Navier–Stokes
  claim, and any 2026 statement by Tenev himself. Zeilberger's "Leiden declaration" reference
  isn't explained on his page.
