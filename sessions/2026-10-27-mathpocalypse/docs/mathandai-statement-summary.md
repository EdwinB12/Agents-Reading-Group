# Summary: "A Severe Misalignment of AI in Mathematics"

- **Source:** <https://mathandai.org/>
- **Published:** 11 September 2026 — DOI [10.5281/zenodo.22737750](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.22737750)
- **Signatories:** 28 Fields Medallists, including Artur Avila, Manjul Bhargava, Pierre Deligne, Martin Hairer, Maxim Kontsevich, Peter Scholze, Terence Tao, Cédric Villani and Maryna Viazovska. The page also invites endorsements from other researchers.

## Core claim

AI companies now use the solving of hard, famous maths problems as a benchmark of LLM progress. The signatories argue that this goal is **severely misaligned** with what the mathematical community is for, and they place it within wider AI alignment problems affecting science, creative work and society.

## Key points

1. **Problems are landmarks, not destinations.** When a famous problem is solved, the community then studies, simplifies and teaches the solution. Training students and passing on ideas are central to the profession.
2. **Understanding over answers.** Solving problems is a means of gaining conceptual understanding. Mass-producing true/false results could weaken the field's ability to generate new ideas.
3. **Rushed announcements and attribution.** AI results are often announced quickly, without full write-ups, proper credit to prior work, or time for the community to absorb the methods. This raises concerns about attribution and plagiarism.
4. **A wider threat to intellectual work.** In many professions, training builds understanding as well as producing outputs. When AI delivers the outputs directly, these two aims come apart.
5. **Not anti-AI.** AI could improve real mathematical study, and the field will need to adapt. How this turns out depends on the decisions of the people who control the technology.

## Call to action

The signatories ask the mathematical community, AI companies and society more broadly to address these issues urgently.
