Re‑Reading the Record

What agents actually contribute when you point them backwards at research that already exists.

Maturity runs opposite to excitement. The most reliable systems do the narrowest, most deterministic things. The boldest claims about autonomous discovery from old work carry the thinnest evidence.
Audit — verify against a knowable standard Discovery — extract unexploited value straddles both

Image & data integrity (side channel)

ImagetwinProofig Problematic Paper ScreenerASM pilot, 2023–

The counterexample to the rest of the tour: narrow task, dedicated (non‑LLM) models, 160M+ figures indexed, 80–90% detection vs. PubPeer cases, already wired into editorial systems.

Deterministic checks (Layer 0)

statcheck, 2016GRIM SPRITEmetacheck

Rule-based, pre-LLM, narrow — and it works at scale. Roughly half of scanned psychology papers had an internally inconsistent p-value. The LLM adds parsing, not judgement.

Text-internal LLM checks (Layer 1)

Black Spatula, 2025"To Err Is Human", 2025

Relatively precise, poor recall: 83% of flags confirmed by hand, but injected-error studies show most real mistakes still go undetected. What it flags is often real; what it misses tells you nothing.

Paper ↔ code alignment (Layer 2)

SciCoQA, 2026scicode-lint, 2026

Precision is worst exactly where the stakes are highest: 68–72% for routine bugs, only 24% for data leakage. Three-quarters of examined papers had at least one verified code defect.

Execution & reproduction (Layer 3)

CORE-Bench, 2024PaperBench REPRO-Bench, 2025Xu & Yang, 2026

Clone it, rebuild it, run it, check the numbers. CORE-Bench's tiers show most of this is really "can anything rebuild the environment" — a decade-old RSE problem the agent inherits, not solves.

Evidence synthesis at scale

otto-SR, 2025

The strongest "discovery" result in the field, and really an audit result in discovery's clothing: twelve Cochrane reviews reproduced and updated in two days, screening beating dual human reviewers, two eligible studies found that the original authors missed.

Retrieval

KnIT, 2014Erdősgate, 2025 Erdős #728/729/397, 2026PaperQA2

Locating results that already exist but are effectively lost. The most reliable value in the field and the most misleading headlines — "solved" routinely turns out to mean "found."

Re-analysis

Kepler-90i, 2017ExoMiner, 2021 Hubble anomaly search, 2026Xu & Yang, 2026

Better methods applied to old, under-exploited data from a single archive. Political-science reproducibility rose from 29.6% to 79.8% once data-availability mandates existed — the bottleneck was data, not agent capability.

Recombination (literature-based discovery)

Swanson, 1986Arrowsmith Tshitoyan et al., 2019AI co-scientist, 2025

The ABC model: A relates to B somewhere, B relates to C elsewhere, so A and C may be related though no paper says so. Formalised by Swanson in 1986; still hard to evaluate — there's no consensus on what counts as a "discovery."

Hypothesis generation

Robin, 2025Virtual Lab, 2025

Weakest ground, most excitement. The July 2026 concentration finding is the sharpest statement of the limit: agents interpolate comfortably within the literature and rarely extrapolate beyond it.

Cross-cutting, both branches

Verification bottleneck

Not how many errors an agent finds — how many flags a human can adjudicate per hour. Security got here first: curl and Google both stopped accepting AI-generated reports in 2026.

Attribution problem

Credit belongs to the pipeline — search, verifier, human problem selection — not "the AI." True from Erdős #728 to the phage-tail mechanism.

Data condition

Kosmos's own reliability tracked input data quality. FAIR compliance isn't overhead alongside adoption — it's what determines whether any of this works.
