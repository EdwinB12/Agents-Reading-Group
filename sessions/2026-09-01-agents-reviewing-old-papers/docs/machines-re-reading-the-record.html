Machines Re-Reading the Record

Six decades of computational systems built not to collect new data, but to re-examine research that already exists — literature, archives, reference databases, prior claims.

Scope

Four ways to re-read a record

This excludes AI applied to fresh data collection or de novo simulation — a different lineage entirely. What follows are systems that extract something new from research that already exists.

01
Recombination
Connecting findings published separately and never linked — Swanson's "undiscovered public knowledge."
02
Re-analysis
Applying better methods to old data that was under-exploited at the time.
03
Error detection
Finding mistakes, inconsistencies, and fabrication in the published record.
04
Retrieval
Locating results that already exist but have been effectively lost.

Mode 4 is the least glamorous — and, on the evidence ahead, the most reliable.

Era 01
1965–99
Rule-based mining of the record
1965–80
DENDRAL / Meta-DENDRAL — first machine to generate publishable domain knowledge from an existing data corpus.
1979–81
BACON — rediscovered Kepler's, Ohm's and Boyle's laws from historical data — and the origin of a lasting critique: pre-cleaned data isn't discovery.
1986
Don Swanson — linked separate literatures on fish oil and blood rheology to predict a Raynaud's treatment. The founding moment.
1997
Arrowsmith — turned Swanson's method into a public tool, the direct ancestor of every modern hypothesis agent.
Era 02
2000–15
Closing the loop, and turning scrutiny on the record
2004–09
Robot Scientist "Adam" — hypothesis → automated experiment → revision, with complete provenance logging still unmatched by most 2026 agents.
2014
KnIT — set the gold-standard evaluation design: truncate the literature, predict what came next, check.
2015
Robot Scientist "Eve" — repurposed triclosan against malaria from existing compound libraries.
2015–16
statcheck — scanned decades of psychology papers; roughly half contained a p-value inconsistency.
Era 03

Deep learning reads the archive

2019
Tshitoyan et al. — embeddings on 3.3M materials-science abstracts predicted thermoelectric materials years before their discovery.
2020–22

Signal, not noise

Six results carry the weight

1986
Swanson — the literature contains unstated implied knowledge. Everything since is engineering.
2014
KnIT — the correct evaluation design: truncate the corpus, predict what came next, check.
2019
Tshitoyan et al. — latent knowledge is recoverable statistically, without any explicit reasoning step.
2025
AlphaEvolve — search over the existing formal record can beat results that stood for half a century.
2025
Kosmos — established the current reliability frontier honestly, including the 58% figure for its own novel syntheses.
2026
Xu & Yang — whole-literature reanalysis has moved from a multi-year project to a workflow.
What the record shows

Five things worth carrying into the room

01
Retrieval is where the reliable value sits — and it's systematically undersold.
Erdősgate was reported as a failure. Recovering forgotten solutions at superhuman recall is genuinely valuable; the failure was purely one of framing.
02
Attribution is structural, not incidental.
Novelty belongs to the pipeline — search process, verifier, human problem selection — not the language model alone. Governance that credits "the AI" will misdescribe what happened.
03
Error detection scales faster than error adjudication.
From statcheck to handbook boiling points, machine re-reading finds problems in volume at 80–85% precision. The bottleneck is human adjudication cost — design that pathway before switching detection on.
04
Agents interpolate; they do not extrapolate.
Re-examining the existing record is exactly where agents perform best — and generating directions the literature doesn't already imply is exactly where they perform worst.
05
Verification asymmetry determines which fields move first.
Maths moved fastest because Lean gives mechanical proof-checking. Fields without cheap verification will see the largest gap between claimed and real progress.
Where this leaves us

Sixty years of re-reading, and the record still keeps giving up more than we've extracted from it.

The consistent pattern: agents excel at finding what's already there, and struggle to point beyond it. That asymmetry is the discussion.
